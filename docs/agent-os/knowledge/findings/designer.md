# Designer findings

Research for the designer agent: what the evidence says, each finding with its
source. The rules file (`../designer.md`) holds the founder's lessons and the
checklist and is read before every task; this file is searched when a task
needs it (grep the `###` headings). No line cap: replace a weaker finding
when a better source arrives, rather than adding beside it.

## What the research says

Strength: strong (peer-reviewed study, official rule or standard), moderate
(platform or company data, industry report with a stated method), practitioner
(blog, trend piece, case study; weak on its own). Researched 2026-11-11.

### Basketball visual culture now

- Nike's biggest campaign of 2025, "Why Do It?", aimed squarely at teen and young athletes and dropped the glossy look for grainy, high-contrast footage, handheld camera, jump cuts and animated type, with failure shown as part of the story (a false start, a missed trick, then the comeback). **For Deadeye:** this is the look the biggest sports brand chose for our age group; raw phone footage, hard contrast and type that moves fit "Aim is earned". Show the miss before the make. [Nike newsroom](https://about.nike.com/en/newsroom/releases/nike-why-do-it-campaign), 2025, strong (official, what was launched); [GDUSA on the visual design](https://gdusa.com/nike-reframes-just-do-it-for-anxious-next-gen-athletes/), 2025, practitioner.
- The NBA's 2025-26 City Edition kits were a "remix" season: each team reworked an earlier City design, with black bases, heritage stories tied to the city (music, Indigenous culture, local landmarks) and collaborations such as Kith with the Knicks. **For Deadeye:** local identity sells to fans; for Irish players lean on our own places (club, county, school, outdoor court) rather than borrowing NBA city looks. Black base plus one strong accent is current. [NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/news/2025-26-nike-city-edition-uniforms-unveiled), 2025, strong (official).
- Young fans now meet basketball mostly through clips: Morning Consult data cited by eMarketer has 18 to 24 year olds watching about 2.7 hours of sports highlights a week, and other 2025-26 surveys find a large share of Gen Z feel closer to individual athletes than to teams. **For Deadeye:** design for the clip and the person, not the league: the player's own made shot, their own number, their name. Every result screen should survive being screen-recorded. [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/nba-finals-ratings-drop-gen-z-turns-clips-over-cable), 2025, moderate (industry survey, US sample).
- House of Highlights was deliberately rebranded by Bleacher Report's design team to feel young, DIY and simple, and Digiday reports its posts are less polished than the parent brand's because so much is user-submitted; B/R's polished branded video did not work there. **For Deadeye:** keep in-app graphics clean, but let share cards and social carry the raw player footage; do not over-produce the player's clip. [Dribbble, B/R design team](https://dribbble.com/shots/5399733-House-of-Highlights-Rebrand), 2018, practitioner, `dated`; [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/bleacher-report-house-highlights/), 2017, moderate, `dated`.
- Sports media social graphics share one house style: heavy sans-serif headline over a cut-out or edited jersey photo, built fast from prepared templates. **For Deadeye:** heavy sans headline plus real photo is the language teens already read; our version is the player's own frame from the camera, not a cut-out of a pro. [Impersonal Foul, interview with a B/R brand designer](https://www.impersonalfoul.com/p/inside-the-sports-media-graphic-design), 2022, practitioner, `dated`.
- Overtime positions itself as a creator brand and lifestyle label more than a rights-holder, building its own leagues and team identities in-house (Overtime Elite teams with names like City Reapers and YNG Dreamerz). **For Deadeye:** teen basketball brands win on personality and named things players can belong to; name levels, drills and challenges with attitude rather than generic labels. [Sports Pundit](https://sportspundit.substack.com/p/different-game-overtimes-ip-empire), 2025, practitioner.
- NBA 2K26 reviews describe the MyCAREER menus as easy to get around (stats, missions and upgrades reachable from the start) but some critics call the UI rushed and short of colour; no menu overhaul was reported. **For Deadeye:** teens know the 2K pattern of a player card with ratings, badges and progress; borrow the idea of a personal "card", not 2K's art or layout. [Gaming Trend review](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/nba-2k26-review-nothing-but-net/), 2025, practitioner.
- Irish basketball's look: Basketball Ireland's 2021 identity is an emerald basketball-and-shamrock emblem with a Gilroy sans wordmark, the 2023-24 national league marks use a half-court drawing, and the Nike national kit (2024-25) draws on the Celtic Dara Knot. **For Deadeye:** emerald and shamrock read as the governing body, so do not use them as our brand colours; an Irish touch can come from places and language, not their marks. [Basketball Ireland rebrand](https://ireland.basketball/basketball-Ireland-rebrand-launch), 2021, strong (official), `dated`; [Basketball Ireland kit](https://ireland.basketball/-basketball-ireland-officially-launch-latest-kit-design), 2024, strong (official).
- European basketball identity work is heritage-led: the EuroLeague Final Four 2026 Athens mark puts the trophy in a ball-shaped shield on a mosaic, and the 2026-27 season brought twenty new club identities. **For Deadeye:** Europe's top level is going classic and crest-like; a teen training app can stand apart with a sharper, street look. [Media Marketing](https://www.media-marketing.com/en/news/euroleague-final-four-2026-in-athens-uses-event-identity-as-a-tool-to-strengthen-european-sporting-heritage/), 2026, practitioner.

### Sports and fitness app UI patterns

- Strava's Year in Sport 2024 was built in-house with Rive for 5.87 million athletes in 14 languages, personalised per athlete, with motion described as fluid and energetic, and got about 2.6 million shares. In 2025 the recap and monthly stat cards moved behind the subscription, which drew complaints. **For Deadeye:** a personal recap of the player's own season is the most shared format in fitness; keep a basic recap free for every player (it is our growth loop) and animate it lightly. [Rive](https://rive.app/blog/strava-year-in-sport-built-with-rive), 2025, moderate (company figures); [road.cc](https://road.cc/content/news/strava-year-sport-now-only-subscribers-317425), 2025, moderate.
- WHOOP's 2025 home screen puts three dials (Sleep, Recovery, Strain) at the top, each opening a deep-dive page that shows what fed the score, with colour bands for good and bad days. **For Deadeye:** one big number per idea, tap for the breakdown; on home show at most three headline stats (e.g. makes today, streak, accuracy) and push detail one level down. [WHOOP](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/the-all-new-whoop-home-screen/), 2025, moderate (company).
- Duolingo's own streak research: the streak is a flame; a milestone animation raised the chance a brand-new learner was still active a week later by 1.7%; letting learners hold two streak freezes instead of one lifted daily active learners; learners with a 7-day streak were 3.6 times more likely to finish a course. Duolingo says loss aversion takes over as the motivator once a streak is long. **For Deadeye:** short, joyful milestone moments at 3, 7 and 30 days work; always offer a freeze; be wary of the loss-aversion phase with under-18s (see the gamification section). [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit), 2022, moderate (company data, no method published), `dated`.
- Nike Run Club's achievements area shows challenge trophies, streaks and personal bests; commentators note it rewards consistency and personal progress, with leaderboards kept secondary, and that shared achievements bring people back. **For Deadeye:** lead with personal bests and consistency; put leaderboards one tap away, not on home. [Appcues GoodUX](https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/nike-run-club-gamification), practitioner.
- HomeCourt, the closest basketball rival, uses the phone camera to count, chart and locate makes and misses live; another shot-chart app uses green for makes and red for misses. **For Deadeye:** our MAKE/MISS overlay must not rely on green and red alone (see accessibility); shape, word or icon must carry the meaning. [HomeCourt](https://www.homecourt.ai/in-app/gettingstarted), moderate (company); [HoopCharts help](https://hoopcharts-pages.pages.dev/help), practitioner.
- Apple Design Awards 2025 winners included Watch Duty (Social Impact) and CapWords (Delight and Fun); no sports-training app won. Google Play's Best App 2025 was Focus Friend, a playful habit app with a character. **For Deadeye:** the award-winning pattern is one clear job, playful character and polish in the small moments, not more features. [Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-unveils-winners-and-finalists-of-the-2025-apple-design-awards/), 2025, strong (official); [Google blog](https://blog.google/products/google-play/best-apps-games-2025/), 2025, strong (official).

### Gamification for teens: what motivates and what manipulates

- A meta-analysis of controlled studies found gamification has small positive effects on learning, motivation and behaviour, larger when it mixes competition with collaboration and adds a story. **For Deadeye:** team challenges and a "season" framing beat lone points; points alone are weak. [Sailer and Homner, Educational Psychology Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w), 2020, strong (peer-reviewed), `dated`.
- In a two-year field study, adding badges made users more active, but an earlier randomised trial in the same service found the lift came mainly from people who actually looked at their own and others' badges. **For Deadeye:** badges only work if players see them; give each badge a moment on screen when earned and a visible shelf, rather than a hidden list. [Hamari, Computers in Human Behavior](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/27336812?lang=en_GB), 2017, strong (peer-reviewed), `dated`.
- An experiment with 111 students put people at the top or bottom of a 10-person leaderboard: those at the bottom worked harder for a few rounds but felt much less competent, and the authors read it as ego-driven effort, not enjoyment. **For Deadeye:** default to small, fair boards (friends, team, same age band) and personal-best views; never show a 13-year-old a long list with them at the bottom. [Na and Han, Internet Research](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/intr-12-2021-0897/full/html), 2023, strong (peer-reviewed).
- A UCL study of habit forming found that missing a single day did not materially harm habit formation. A review of 859 streak designs found almost all count days, many lean on fear (countdowns, late-night warnings), and forgiving designs (freezes, pauses, repairs) are spreading. **For Deadeye:** a missed day should never feel like a disaster; use a flame that dims rather than one that dies, offer freezes, no countdown clocks, no late-night streak warnings. [Lally et al., European Journal of Social Psychology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674), 2010, strong (peer-reviewed), `dated`; [Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/blog/streaks-are-about-losing-a-study-of-859-streak-designs), 2026, practitioner.
- UK Children's Code, Standard 13: do not use nudges (one option much bigger, rosier framing, the privacy option made slower) to push children to share more data or weaken privacy; nudges towards wellbeing, such as breaks, are encouraged, and for 13 and over the code suggests signposting support and wellbeing behaviours. **For Deadeye:** "Yes" and "No" on any data or sharing choice get equal size and weight; orange is for the next training action, never for "share more". [ICO Standard 13](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/13-nudge-techniques/), strong (official code).
- UK Children's Code, Standard 5: data-driven features that make it hard for children to stop (built on reward, anticipation or peer pressure) are likely unfair; good practice is to present "keep going" neutrally without implying loss, require an active choice to continue, and offer pauses that do not cost progress. **For Deadeye:** end-of-session screens say "Done for today" as plainly as "Go again"; no "you'll lose your streak" copy; a streak pause (holiday, injury) that keeps the count. [ICO Standard 5](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/5-detrimental-use-of-data/), strong (official code).
- The ICO's tips for game designers suggest break prompts and checkpoints, and say themed or game-styled break prompts can work better with children; optional data uses should be off by default. The ICO notes the tips are under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. **For Deadeye:** design a branded "rest day" or "recovery" card that feels like part of the game, not a warning. [ICO tips for games designers](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/childrens-code-hub/top-tips-for-games-designers-how-to-comply-with-the-children-s-code), 2023, moderate (official but non-binding).
- Ireland's DPC "Fundamentals" apply to anyone under 18, list 14 Fundamentals (including a precautionary approach to profiling and data protection "baked in" by design and default), and, like the UK code, say nudges must not push children to give unnecessary data or switch off protections. **For Deadeye:** Ireland is our first market, so the DPC applies directly; settings screens default to the most private option and the designer never styles the less private choice as the hero. [DPC Fundamentals](https://www.dataprotection.ie/sites/default/files/uploads/2021-12/Fundamentals%20for%20a%20Child-Oriented%20Approach%20to%20Data%20Processing_FINAL_EN.pdf), 2021, strong (official regulator), `dated`; [Arthur Cox comparison of DPC and ICO](https://arthurcox.com/knowledge/childrens-data-a-comparison-of-the-dpc-and-icos-approaches), practitioner.
- The EU Commission's 2025 guidelines on protecting minors under the Digital Services Act recommend switching off by default features that drive excessive use, naming communication "streaks", autoplay, push notifications and virtual rewards tied to engagement. They are not binding but are the Commission's benchmark for compliance. **For Deadeye:** treat streak reminders and push nudges as opt-in for under-18s; the streak itself should reward training, not just opening the app. [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors), 2025, strong (official guidance).
- The EDPB's guidelines on deceptive design patterns list patterns that breach GDPR (overloading, skipping, stirring emotions, hindering, fickle and left-in-the-dark interfaces) and recommend designers and data protection people review designs together. **For Deadeye:** use the EDPB list as a checklist when reviewing any sign-up, consent or sharing screen. [EDPB Guidelines 03/2022](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-032022-deceptive-design-patterns-social-media_en), 2023, strong (official), `dated`.

### Shareable graphics and stat cards

- Instagram's official Sharing to Stories feature takes a background (minimum 720x1280, 9:16 or 9:18 recommended) and/or a sticker image, plus top and bottom background colours; video backgrounds can be 1080p and up to 20 seconds. **For Deadeye:** build the share card as a 1080x1920 background with the stat card as a sticker layer, so players can move and resize it; set both gradient colours to our zinc so empty space matches. [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/sharing-to-stories), strong (official).
- Meta does not publish a single pixel safe zone for organic Stories; the widely repeated figures are community measurements, roughly 250px clear at the top and 340px or more at the bottom of 1080x1920, with some guides saying far more at the bottom for ads. TikTok's own help says its safe zone depends on caption length and gives downloadable overlay files. **For Deadeye:** keep the number, name and logo inside the central band (about y 270 to 1500, x 65 to 1015) and test every template under the real Instagram and TikTok overlays before shipping. [Socialync safe-zone tool](https://www.socialync.io/tools/safe-zones/instagram-stories), practitioner; [TikTok help, TopView specs](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-reservation-topview), strong (official).
- Instagram's head said in 2025 that the top three ranking signals are watch time, likes and sends, and that the rate per reach matters more than the count; sends weigh slightly more for reaching non-followers. **For Deadeye:** design cards people want to send to a teammate (a challenge, a beat-this number), not just post. [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/), 2025, moderate (official, reported).
- A study of about 7,000 New York Times articles plus experiments found content that triggers high-arousal emotions (awe, excitement, also anger and anxiety) is shared more than low-arousal content, and positive content more than negative. **For Deadeye:** share cards should capture a peak (new best, streak milestone, 10 in a row), not an average session. [Berger and Milkman, Journal of Marketing Research](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf), 2012, strong (peer-reviewed), `dated`.
- Commentary on Spotify Wrapped says people share it as a safe way to show identity and find their crowd, helped by oversized type and bright fields that look good as a screenshot; no peer-reviewed Wrapped study was found. **For Deadeye:** the card should say something about the player ("Deadeye from the corner", "Free-throw machine"), with one giant number and minimal text. [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-12-06-spotify-wrapped-is-about-more-than-what-songs-you-listen-to-its-about-what-makes-you-you), 2024, practitioner.

### Social graphics trends for sports brands

- Canva's 2026 trends report (design and search data plus a survey of 1,000 creators in the US and Brazil) named 2026 "Imperfect by Design", with searches for DIY and collage elements up 90%. **For Deadeye:** hand-made touches (tape, scribbled circles on a shot, grain) fit the moment; keep them on social and share cards, not in the core app UI. [Business Wire, Canva](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251210696597/en), 2025, moderate (industry, method stated).
- 2026 trend round-ups point to grain and scanned textures, bold type borrowed from sports and street culture, and type collage that trades legibility for energy. **For Deadeye:** grain and collage for posters and promo; numbers and stats always stay crisp and readable. [Spreadshirt 2026 trends](https://www.spreadshirt.com/custom/blog/2026-design-trends/), 2026, practitioner.
- TikTok's own creative guidance asks for a native, not over-polished look, the point made in the first seconds, and several versions tested. Agency write-ups add that "ugly" or lo-fi ads work only when the rawness is authentic, and that polished product shots still help at the point of conversion. **For Deadeye:** raw player footage for the hook; one clean, sharp app screen for the proof. [TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en), 2025, strong (official); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ugly-ads-outperforming-polished-creative-475007), 2026, practitioner.
- Bleacher Report's football graphics package mixed rough graffiti and tag textures with splashes of clean flat design. **For Deadeye:** the pairing of one rough layer with clean type is a proven sports-media formula; do not make everything rough. [NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2018/10/08/br-live-graphics-package/), 2018, practitioner, `dated`.

### App store screenshots and icons

- Google Play rules: icon 512x512 PNG with no badges or ranking text; feature graphic 1024x500 with key content centred and no pure white, black or dark grey backgrounds (they vanish against the store); 2 to 8 screenshots per device, at least four 9:16 (1080x1920 minimum) to be eligible for larger promotion; real in-app footage; UI in the first three; taglines no more than 20% of the image; no "best", "#1", "free" or "download now". **For Deadeye:** zinc-950 cannot be the feature graphic background; use orange or a lit court photo. Captions short, under 20% of each frame. [Google Play Console help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151), strong (official).
- Apple rules: up to 10 screenshots, and up to three appear in search results depending on orientation; show the real experience, lead with the strongest feature, tell a story across the set; previews autoplay muted; assets must suit a 4+ rating; no unverifiable claims or other platforms' logos. **For Deadeye:** screenshot 1 is the live MAKE/MISS camera overlay, 2 the streak or stats, 3 the share card; first three must make sense alone. [Apple asset best practices](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/asset-best-practices/), strong (official).
- Both stores let you A/B test store creative: Google Play's store listing experiments test icons, screenshots and video and report installs and 1-day retention; Apple's product page optimisation tests alternative visuals. **For Deadeye:** test one change at a time (first screenshot first) once traffic allows. [Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/distribute/best-practices/grow/store-listing-experiments), strong (official).
- Vendor tests suggest the first screenshots carry most of the weight: moving a key screen to first place lifted installs 6.4% in one test, and few visitors scroll past the second screenshot. **For Deadeye:** spend most of the design effort on screenshot 1. [SplitMetrics](https://splitmetrics.com/blog/apple-screenshots-scroll-depth/), 2018, practitioner (vendor case), `dated`.

### Accessibility and dark mode

- WCAG 2.2 added nine criteria, including Focus Not Obscured (AA: a focused item must not be fully hidden, for example by a sticky bar), Target Size Minimum (AA: 24x24 CSS px or enough spacing), Dragging Movements (AA: give a non-drag option) and Accessible Authentication (AA: no memory puzzles at login). **For Deadeye:** the bottom nav and sticky "Log shot" bar must never cover a focused field; every swipe action (e.g. dismiss a shot) needs a button too. [W3C, What's new in WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/), 2023, strong (official standard).
- Platform touch targets are bigger than the WCAG floor: Apple recommends 44x44pt and Material 48x48dp with about 8dp between targets; Google's accessibility help gives the same guidance for Android. **For Deadeye:** 48x48 minimum for every tappable thing, more for in-session buttons used with sweaty hands mid-drill. [Google Accessibility help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858), strong (official); [Material layout density](https://material.io/design/layout/applying-density.html), strong (official).
- WCAG Use of Color: colour cannot be the only way information is shown; about 1 in 12 men have a colour vision deficiency, mostly red-green. **For Deadeye:** MAKE and MISS must differ by word, icon (tick/cross or ring/no ring) and lightness, not just green against red; check charts with a deuteranopia simulator. [W3C Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG20/Understanding/use-of-color), strong (official standard), `dated`.
- WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) and its technique C39: honour the reduced-motion setting and remove motion that is not essential, because movement can cause dizziness and nausea for people with vestibular conditions; reduced does not have to mean none (fades are fine). **For Deadeye:** confetti, flame bursts and count-ups switch to a simple fade under `prefers-reduced-motion`. [W3C Understanding 2.3.3](https://w3c.github.io/wcag/understanding/animation-from-interactions.html), strong (official standard).
- Apple's dark mode guidance: aim for at least 4.5:1 contrast and 7:1 for custom colour pairs, especially small text; dark palettes are not simple inversions; test with Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency on, where dark text on dark surfaces often fails. **For Deadeye:** small grey labels on zinc-900 are our biggest risk; check every grey on every surface and in the light theme too. [Apple HIG Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode), strong (official).
- Material's dark theme advice uses dark grey (#121212) rather than pure black so shadows and elevation read, with lighter surfaces for higher layers; Material 3 replaced the overlays with tone-based surface roles (container low, container, container high). **For Deadeye:** our zinc-950/900/800 steps already follow this; keep raised cards one step lighter rather than adding shadows. [Material dark theme (M2)](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme), strong (official), `dated`; [Material blog, tone-based surfaces](https://material.io/blog/tone-based-surface-color-m3), 2023, strong (official), `dated`.
- NN/g's review of the research: people with normal vision read better in light mode, clearly so in dark rooms, with little difference in daylight; designers should still offer dark mode because some users with low vision do better with it and many prefer it. **For Deadeye:** keep both themes working; long text (privacy notices, coach notes) must read well in light mode. [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/), 2020, moderate (research review), `dated`.

### Design critique method and tools

- Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics (visibility of status, match with the real world, user control, consistency, error prevention, recognition over recall, flexibility, minimalist design, error recovery, help) are unchanged since 1994 and were reworded in 2020. **For Deadeye:** score each new screen against the ten, plus our own brand checks, and note the worst three problems first. [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), 2020, strong (standard method), `dated`.
- NN/g separates a design critique (a group looks at in-progress work against its stated goal) from heuristic evaluation and wider expert reviews; good critiques state the goal first, keep to agreed rules and end with owners for each action. **For Deadeye:** every review opens with "this screen's job is…", and feedback ties back to that job and to a named player type (new 13-year-old, coach). [NN/g, design reviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-expert-reviews/), moderate.
- The W3C Design Tokens Community Group published the first stable Design Tokens specification (2025.10), a shared JSON format for colours, spacing and type; Figma can import tokens in this format. **For Deadeye:** keep our colour and spacing decisions as DTCG-format tokens that map to the Tailwind/CSS variables, so code and any Figma file stay in step. [W3C DTCG announcement](https://www.w3.org/community/design-tokens/2025/10/28/design-tokens-specification-reaches-first-stable-version/), 2025, strong (official standard).
- Figma variables hold modes (light and dark), can alias other variables to form semantic tokens, and a new theme is just a new mode. **For Deadeye:** if a Figma file is made, use one primitives collection and one semantic collection with light and dark modes matching `docs/design.md`. [Figma help, modes](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/15343816063383), strong (official tool docs).

### Legal and intellectual property

- The NBA's terms say NBA Properties owns or licenses all league and team marks, and unauthorised use may break trademark, copyright, privacy and publicity laws; its data-partner terms bar implying any link with the NBA, teams or players. **For Deadeye:** no NBA, WNBA or team logos, kit designs, wordmarks or player images anywhere, including drill names and share cards; describe moves generically ("step-back three"). [NBA.com terms of use](https://www.nba.com/termsofuse), strong (official); [NBA addendum, Sportradar](https://developer.sportradar.com/NBA_Addendum), moderate.
- English courts held a radio station liable for passing off when it doctored a photo to suggest a racing driver endorsed it, and raised the damages on appeal to £25,000. Ireland has no statutory image right, but passing off and data protection can apply. **For Deadeye:** never use a real athlete's image or name in a way that suggests endorsement, and never use a player's (especially a child's) photo in marketing without written consent; get Irish legal advice before any athlete tie-in. [Irvine v Talksport, Court of Appeal](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2003/423), 2003, strong (court ruling), `dated`.
- Geist, our font, is released under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, which allows commercial use, change and redistribution; Google Fonts are also mostly OFL, may be used in commercial products and in logos, but the font files themselves cannot be sold. **For Deadeye:** use Geist or other OFL fonts for app, share cards and promo; check the licence before adding any new display face (many "sports" and distressed fonts on free sites are personal-use only). [Vercel, Geist](https://vercel.com/font/geist), strong (official licence); [Google Fonts FAQ](https://developers.google.com/fonts/faq), strong (official).
- Apple's store rules ban other platforms' logos and unverifiable claims in store assets, and Google Play's ban unlicensed third-party trademarks in the feature graphic. **For Deadeye:** no Nike swoosh, Jordan Jumpman or team logo on screenshots, including on clothes in lifestyle shots; blur or reshoot. [Apple asset best practices](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/asset-best-practices/), strong (official); [Google Play Console help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151), strong (official).
- The US Copyright Office's 2025 report confirms that purely AI-generated material is not protected by copyright; only human-authored parts are. **For Deadeye:** do not build the logo, mascot or core brand art from AI output alone, or we may not own it; AI is fine for drafts and mood boards that a person then redraws. [US Copyright Office, via Library of Congress blog](https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2025/02/inside-the-copyright-offices-report-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence-part-2-copyrightability/), 2025, strong (official, US law).
- The EU AI Act's transparency duties (Article 50) applied from 2 August 2026, with a Commission-endorsed code of practice on marking and labelling AI-generated content; deployers must label deepfakes. **For Deadeye:** never publish AI-made images or video of realistic people playing basketball as if real; label any AI-generated visual in marketing and prefer real footage. [European Commission, code of practice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content), 2026, strong (official).
