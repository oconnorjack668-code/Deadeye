## Checklist

### Outreach drafts

- [ ] Addressed to the club's public channel or a role (secretary, chair, underage
      coordinator, DSCO); never a private person's name or details.
- [ ] 50 to 125 words, plain words, one ask (a short chat, or one coach trying one team),
      at most two questions; a short subject line naming the club, town or age group.
- [ ] Opens with a real local reason (players nearby, the area board, Wexford results
      once there are some). Warm route looked for first.
- [ ] Safeguarding stated exactly as built: head coaches 18+ and confirm Garda vetting; no
      private messages, coaches post to the whole team; under-16s need a parent's approval
      and those parents see coach posts. Never says or hints that BI approves Deadeye.
- [ ] Features and prices checked against the app, `/pro` and
      `docs/sales/deadeye-club-plans.html`; founding offer quoted whole (first 3 clubs,
      4+ teams, free to 31 Jan 2027, then €59.99 a team a season, closes 31 Dec 2026).
- [ ] No pressure, no "everyone else has signed up", no fake deadlines, no rival named.
- [ ] Follow-up planned: at most two, about a week apart, then stop. The founder sends.
- [ ] Timing checked against the season calendar below.

### Trial plans

- [ ] One team first: head coach as champion, a committee contact (chair or underage
      coordinator) who knows it is running, and the DSCO told in writing before players join.
- [ ] Two or three success criteria readable from the Monday counts, and a decision date.
- [ ] Written: the plan and `/pro` price if it works, a clean stop if it does not.
- [ ] Parent message for under-16s ready, ideally handed out where parents are present.
- [ ] No exports, club-wide view of children's data or sharing with a school promised;
      any such request goes to the founder.
- [ ] Adds almost no admin for the coach; the founder offers to help set teams up.

## What the research says

### How volunteer clubs choose and adopt tools

- Clubs use digital tools mostly for communication (94%) and federation reporting (82%);
  use depends on fit with club culture, volunteers' admin load, a plan, and money. **For
  Deadeye:** show it fits what coaches already do; never pitch a new system to run.
  [Ehnold et al.](https://doi.org/10.1108/SBM-10-2019-0081), 2021, strong (787 clubs, AT/DE).
- Clubs differ in attitude: some see digital change as risky, some are open but want lots
  of support. **For Deadeye:** ask early how the club feels about apps; lead a cautious club
  with safeguarding and setup help. [Ehnold, Steinbach, Schlesinger](https://doi.org/10.1080/23750472.2023.2224343),
  2023, moderate (abstract only).
- Club decisions are mostly reactive, made by a few dominant people through committee and
  informal networks. **For Deadeye:** find who really decides and the problem they have
  now; an informal yes comes before the committee's.
  [Schlesinger, Klenk, Nagel](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.smr.2014.04.003), 2015, moderate (9 clubs).
- A community club's tech change rested on committed leaders, keen individuals, capacity,
  a simple structure and an involved outside party. **For Deadeye:** coach champion,
  supportive chair, founder as outside helper.
  [Hoeber and Hoeber](https://doi.org/10.1123/jsm.26.3.213), 2012, moderate (one case).
- New ideas in clubs come from board members, members and parents. **For Deadeye:** one
  coach or parent can carry it in; give them the one-page club plan.
  [Hoeber et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/16184742.2015.1085070), 2015, moderate (16 presidents; summaries).
- Adult sport volunteering in Ireland fell from 12% (2019) to 9% (2022); about one in four
  earlier club volunteers may not have returned. **For Deadeye:** keep every ask small.
  [Sport Ireland policy, via Sport for Business](https://sportforbusiness.com/sport-ireland-launches-new-volunteer-policy/),
  2024, moderate (official figures reported second-hand).
- WhatsApp groups are the most used parent-coach channel but not rated most effective.
  **For Deadeye:** clubs keep WhatsApp for logistics; pitch training between sessions, not
  a replacement. [Eckardt et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/1612197X.2025.2525244), 2025,
  strong (1,024 parents, 340 coaches, youth football, Germany).

### Irish youth basketball: structure, money and calendar

- BI lists 223 clubs and about 40,000 members.
  [BI Child Safeguarding Statement](https://ireland.basketball/uploads/ed/BI%20-%20Child%20Safeguarding%20Statement%202024_1.pdf), 2024, strong.
- Clubs pay BI €175 a season (2025/26) and €290 to €520 liability cover by size; player
  fees in 2024/25 were €15 (U18), €5 (U13), free (U10). **For Deadeye:** the founding
  price sits beside costs clubs know; compare plainly. [Registration](https://ireland.basketball/registration),
  [insurance](https://ireland.basketball/insurance), [2024/25 prices](https://ireland.basketball/-basketball-ireland-announce-registration-pricing-for-2024-25-season),
  2024-25, moderate (insurance and player fees from search summaries).
- Every club needed the Bronze Club Mark (basic governance, policies) to register for
  2025/26. **For Deadeye:** ask for the club's safeguarding statement and DSCO at the first
  chat. [BI club governance](https://ireland.basketball/20231122105830-club-governance-update), 2023, strong.
- Registration (Sport:80) opens early August; season launch late September; leagues
  October to March; national cup finals January; area board cup finals March; All-Ireland
  club championships April-May. **For Deadeye (inference):** approach in Aug-Sept or early
  January; start trials Oct-Nov or after cup week; avoid December and finals weeks.
  [BI and Sport:80](https://ireland.basketball/-basketball-ireland-announce-digital-partnership-with-sport-80),
  [calendar 2025/26](https://ireland.basketball/uploads/ed/The%20National%20Basketball%20Calendar%2025.26%20Season.pdf), 2025, strong.
- From 2026/27 every coach needs a BI licence: Introduction to Coaching, Garda vetting and
  Safeguarding 1 (each renewed every 3 years), checked at games; from 2027/28 head coaches
  at U16+ need Level 1. BI vetting does not transfer from other bodies. **For Deadeye:**
  coaches carry heavy admin this season, so add none; the app's vetting declaration is not
  a BI licence, never imply it is. [Minimum standards](https://www.ireland.basketball/coaching-minimum-standards),
  [standards sheet](https://ireland.basketball/uploads/ed/Coaching%20Resources/Minimum%20Standard%20Levels%202026-27%20(Branded).pdf),
  [e-vetting](https://ireland.basketball/e-vetting), 2025-26, strong.

### Safeguarding and data, where they touch an app

- BI's coach policy for minors bans coach-minor direct messages on every platform, wants
  contact in groups that include other adults (parents, administrators) where possible,
  email with a parent copied for anything else, no friend or follow requests, written
  parental consent for images, and "official channels designated by BI". **For Deadeye:**
  no private messages, team-wide posts and no coach to under-18 friendships match; the gap
  is that parents of 16 and 17 year olds cannot see coach posts yet (coach-side handoff).
  [BI Social Media Use Policy for Coaches of Minors](https://ireland.basketball/uploads/ed/BI%20Social%20Media%20Use%20Policy%20for%20Coaches%20of%20Minors_2025.pdf), 2025, strong.
- BI's club role is the Designated Safeguarding Children Officer (DSCO), merging designated
  liaison and children's officer; it reports to the committee and runs parents' sessions at
  the start of the season. Leaders use group messages, not constant one-to-one contact;
  filming needs permission. **For Deadeye:** say "DSCO" to BI clubs; the DSCO is the gate
  before a trial; explain that tracking runs on the phone and videos stay there unless a
  player uploads one. [BI Safeguarding Policy](https://ireland.basketball/uploads/ed/Basketball%20Ireland%20Safeguarding%20Policy%20March%202023%20-%20FINAL_1.pdf), 2023, strong.
- BI's risk register names inappropriate online contact with under-18s and unauthorised
  filming. **For Deadeye:** a club judges a camera app on these two; answer them unasked.
  [BI Child Safeguarding Statement](https://ireland.basketball/uploads/ed/BI%20-%20Child%20Safeguarding%20Statement%202024_1.pdf), 2024, strong.
- DPC survey of 100+ Irish clubs: 41% no data protection policies, 56% no retention
  schedule, 39% collect performance data (likely health data over time), 9% did a DPIA, a
  third use personal devices. **For Deadeye:** clubs are weak on data and the regulator is
  watching; lead with "coaches see weekly totals only"; data requests go to the founder.
  [DPC sports survey](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/latest-news/top-findings-dpc-sports-survey), 2024, strong.

### Outreach to small organisations and volunteers

- Emails of 50 to 125 words got most replies (over 50%); simple wording beat complex; one
  to three questions beat none; 3-4 word subject lines did best. **For Deadeye:** short,
  plain, one ask. [Boomerang](https://blog.boomerangapp.com/?p=3933), 2016, moderate,
  dated (40m emails of all kinds).
- Of 12m outreach emails 8.5% got a reply; one follow-up lifted replies ~66%, three or more
  messages did best; personalised text did about a third better. **For Deadeye:** follow up,
  but cap it at two for volunteers. [Backlinko and Pitchbox](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study),
  2019, moderate, dated (link-building outreach).
- Warm introductions beat cold messages, but every figure found is a vendor's with no
  method. **For Deadeye:** seek a warm route (coaches the founder knows, the area board,
  families on Deadeye); never quote a number for it. Vendor blogs, weak.

### Trials and turning them into paid plans

- In a software field experiment a 7-day free trial beat a 30-day one on sign-ups,
  retention and revenue; beginners did better with short trials. **For Deadeye:** short
  trials with a decision date; the founding offer runs to 31 Jan 2027, so set check-ins at
  weeks 2 and 4 inside it. [Yoganarasimhan, Barzegary, Pani](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4507),
  2023, strong (one firm, 2015 data, not clubs).
- Practitioners agree: 2-3 measurable criteria agreed up front, a decision date, paid terms
  written in advance, and the budget holder involved, not only the champion. No controlled
  study found. [Bright Eye VC](https://www.brighteyevc.com/sidekick-posts/designing-and-building-pilots-that-convert), practitioner, weak.

### Other coach and team apps (facts only)

- Spond is free and earns from fees on payments taken through it
  ([help](https://help.spond.com/en/articles/25998-spond-is-free-to-download-and-use),
  [contact](https://www.spond.com/contact/), moderate). Teamer is free for team management
  with a payment fee ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/160000/teamer), weak).
  **For Deadeye:** clubs expect admin and chat apps free; the free coach tier fits, and the
  paid part is player training (Pro).
- ClubZap (Irish): about 1,000 clubs in 25 sports; quote-based packages, card fees from
  3.75% + 10p, free onboarding, trials case by case, mostly annual billing. **For
  Deadeye:** Irish clubs expect to be set up by the supplier. [ClubZap pricing](https://clubzap.com/?p=877),
  [Think Business](https://www.thinkbusiness.ie/articles/clubzap-sports-club-management-social-ecommerce-app/), moderate.
- HomeCourt: a team is free; a team subscription gives every player Premium (plans for 10
  to 50 players); coaches invite by link. The closest match to the Team Pass.
  [HomeCourt teams FAQ](https://homecourt.ai/faq/teams), moderate.
- Hudl club basketball (US): $400, $1,000 or $1,600 a team a year, club-wide by quote
  ([Hudl pricing](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/club/basketball), moderate). TeamSnap: per
  team, clubs by quote ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.ca/reviews/123208/teamsnap), weak).
  **Pricing for Deadeye:** per team per season plus a club deal is the usual shape; the
  Team Pass, Club plan and 1 Sept season fit it. No evidence found on founding offers.

## How a club trial should run

1. **Before:** the founder talks with the head coach and a committee contact; the DSCO
   gets the safeguarding facts in writing. Agree one team, start date, 2-3 criteria, a
   decision date, and the plan and price if it works.
2. **Day 0:** the head coach (18+, vetting confirmed) sets up and shares the code, QR code
   or link; under-16 parents get the app's parent message, best where parents are present.
3. **Week 1:** most of the team logs a first session; the coach posts one assignment.
4. **Week 2:** second assignment; the founder checks counts and asks the coach what is stuck.
5. **Week 4:** decision with the committee contact: continue, add teams, or stop. Written.

"It worked" means, for example: most of the team logged a first session within 7 days,
the coach posted an assignment each week, and players still train between sessions in
weeks 3 and 4.
