# Video craft playbook

The video editor's long reference: research findings with sources, the craft
playbook, a delivery recipe and open questions. The short version (lessons,
checklist) is `docs/agent-os/knowledge/video-editor.md`. Replace weaker lines
when better sources turn up; don't add beside them.

Researched 2026-11-10 for the knowledge file of the "video-editor" agent. Source types: `study` (peer-reviewed or academic), `official` (platform, regulator or standards body), `industry` (large study with a stated method, often commissioned by a platform), `practitioner` (trade press, vendor or creator write-up). `dated` means platform advice older than about 2 years. Figures marked "secondary" were read in a summary, not the primary document.

## What the research says

### 1. Pacing and retention
- The best direct evidence on editing pace in short-form: 50 high-performing informative talking-head TikToks averaged 0.37 cuts/s (16th/50th/84th percentiles 0.19/0.33/0.52). In an experiment (N=242), seamless jump cuts (silences removed, flow kept) raised liking only at medium or high pace; overlapping cuts raised intent to finish or rewatch at low or medium pace; the fastest pace lowered sustained engagement. **Deadeye:** in talky or explainer stretches, cut every 2-3 s, remove dead air cleanly, and don't speed up for its own sake. [Dost & Huang](https://archives.marketing-trends-congress.com/2026/pages/PDF/paper_professor_DOST_HUANG.pdf), 2026, study (conference paper, convenience sample, one 20 s clip, self-reported intent).
- Hollywood average shot length fell from about 8-10 s (1960s) to 3-4 s (2005), and shot lengths in modern films vary in a 1/f pattern: a mix of short and long shots rather than an even beat. **Deadeye:** vary shot lengths (some quick, some held) and hold the payoff shot longest. [Cutting, DeLong & Nothelfer](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610361679), 2010, study (150 films).
- TikTok advises introducing the content proposition in the first 3 s and landing the hook within the first 6 s. **Deadeye:** the player, the tracking overlay and a MAKE banner should all be on screen within the first 1-2 s. [TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices), updated 2025, official.
- Meta says Reels should "nail the hook within the first few seconds" and names three hook types: a value promise, a statement of intent, and a question. It reports that younger audiences consume content about 3x faster than older ones. **Deadeye:** open with one of the three, in text and voice, over moving footage. [Social Media Today on Meta's tips](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182), 2025, industry (report of Meta guidance, no method given).
- A Facebook-commissioned Nielsen analysis (173 brand-lift studies) found much of a video ad's brand effect arrives in views of 3 s or less (up to 47% of value in the first 3 s, 74% in the first 10 s; sources word this differently). **Deadeye:** brand and app name must be readable early, not saved for an end card. [Marketing Dive](https://marketingdive.com/news/brand-lift-happens-in-less-than-1-second-of-video-study-finds/377333), 2016, industry `dated`.
- The often-quoted "21-34 s TikTok ads get 1.6% more impressions" comes from TikTok data reported second-hand and can't be traced to a primary page. **Deadeye:** don't treat any length as proven; 9-25 s sits inside every platform's limits and Apple's 30 s preview cap. [Marin Software](https://www.marinsoftware.com/blog/how-to-nail-your-tiktok-ad-creative), about 2022, practitioner `dated`.

### 2. Motion: cuts on action, slow motion, zoom, reframing
- Viewers miss about a quarter of cuts between two views of the same scene, and about a third when the cut lands on sudden motion. Match-action cuts were missed 32.4% of the time. **Deadeye:** to hide a cut, cut mid-movement (the release, the ball in flight). [Smith & Henderson](https://doi.org/10.16910/jemr.2.2.6), 2008, study.
- Removing motion after a cut made the cut easier to spot. Most of the "edit blindness" disappeared when the soundtrack was removed, so continuous sound across a cut does much of the hiding. **Deadeye:** keep one ambience or music bed running across cuts. To make a cut felt (the jump to the MAKE replay), change the sound at the cut too. [Smith & Martin-Portugues Santacreu](https://eprints.bbk.ac.uk/id/eprint/14904), 2017, study.
- Slow motion makes an action look more deliberate and intentional, including a sports clip. Showing the real-speed version as well reduced the bias but did not remove it. **Deadeye:** play the shot at real speed first, then a slow-mo replay of the release, so it reads as skill and stays honest. [Caruso, Burns & Converse, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4995977), 2016, study.
- Zoom has a hard limit: a punch-in is sharp only while the cropped area still has at least as many pixels as the output. A 1080x1920 phone clip goes soft at any punch-in. A vertical 4K clip (2160x3840) allows up to 2x. A 16:9 1080p clip cropped to 9:16 leaves 608x1080, a 1.78x upscale. **Deadeye:** ask for vertical 4K when a punch-in is planned. On 1080p sources keep punch-ins small (about 1.2x or less is a rule of thumb, not a tested figure). This is arithmetic, not a study.
- Apple asks app previews to capture the native UI resolution rather than zooming into the view. **Deadeye:** to show the in-app overlay, use the app's own screen recording at native resolution, not a crop of a crop. [Apple app previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/), current, official.
- Speed ramps (fast-slow-fast within one clip) are a standard sports convention for a jump's peak or a moment of impact. They need enough real frames at the slow end, and editors pair them with a whoosh or riser timed to the ramp. **Deadeye:** ramp only on high-frame-rate footage. 60 fps slowed to 50% gives 30 real frames per second. Slowing 30 fps footage stutters unless frames are interpolated. [Freevisuals ramp notes](https://www.freevisuals.net/templates/15-speed-ramp-transitions-premiere-pro), practitioner; frame arithmetic is mine.

### 3. Captions and on-screen text
- In a US survey, 92% of mobile viewers said they sometimes watch video with the sound off. Captions on ads lifted ad recall 8% and brand linkage 13%. Facebook's 2016 tests found captions raised view time by about 12%. **Deadeye:** every claim must work sound-off, in text. [Verizon Media/Publicis via NextTV](https://nexttv.com/news/mobile-videos-often-watched-without-audio-study-finds), 2019, industry `dated`; [Martech on Facebook](https://martech.org/facebook-releases-new-video-ad-features-including-automated-captions), 2016, industry `dated`.
- On TikTok, 88% of users called sound essential and 73% said they would stop and look at ads with audio (TikTok-commissioned Kantar and Nielsen work). **Deadeye:** design sound-on as well: SFX and music carry the energy, text carries the message. [TikTok/Kantar](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok), about 2021, industry `dated`.
- Reading-speed limits: Netflix English allows 17 characters/s for children's programmes (20 for adults), 42 characters per line and two lines at most, with breaks at natural phrase boundaries (never between article and noun, or name and surname). Ofcom's code used 160-180 words/min. A study it cites found about 145 wpm most comfortable and over 180 hard for everyone. **Deadeye:** any line a 13-year-old must read should stay at or under about 17 characters/s (about 3 words/s). [Netflix style guide](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide), current, official; [Ofcom access code](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0037/179956/tv-access-services-code.pdf), 2019, official (Ofcom's 2024 [statement](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/category-1-10-weeks/264291-ensuring-the-quality-of-tv-and-on-demand-access-services/associated-documents/statement-on-ensuring-the-quality-of-tv-and-on-demand-services.pdf?v=356711) dropped fixed figures from its guidelines).
- TikTok's ad guidance says "displaying 5-10 words per second when using text". That is 2-3 times faster than the subtitle standards above. **Deadeye:** use fast word-by-word text only for words that are also spoken aloud. Text that must be read on its own follows the slower limit. [TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices), 2025, official.
- Contrast: WCAG asks for 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold, which is about 24 px or 18.5 px). The rule covers images of text. **Deadeye:** video backgrounds change every frame, so give every caption a stroke, shadow or plate that keeps 4.5:1 on its worst frame. [WCAG 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), current, official.
- Meta's official Reels ad spec says to keep 14% top, 35% bottom and 6% each side free of text, logos and key elements. On 1080x1920 that leaves x 65-1015, y 269-1248. TikTok publishes downloadable safe-zone files and says the zone changes with caption length and add-ons. **Deadeye:** keep all text, the counter and the MAKE/MISS banner inside the stricter of the two zones. [Meta Reels ad spec](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels), current, official; [TikTok ad specs](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/video-ads-specifications?lang=en), updated June 2026, official.
- Flashing: content passes if it has no more than 3 general flashes and no more than 3 red flashes in any 1 s, or if the flashing area is small enough (about 25% of a 10-degree field, roughly a 341x256 area at 1024x768). A general flash is a 10%+ change in relative luminance where the darker state is below 0.80. Saturated red counts on its own. Ofcom applies the same 3-per-second limit. ITU-R BT.1702-3 adds that flashing lasting more than 5 s can be risky even when it passes. **Deadeye:** a red MISS banner must not strobe, and white-flash transitions and fast count tickers must stay at 3 or fewer per second. [WCAG 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html), official; [ITU-R BT.1702-3](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1702-3-202311-I!!PDF-E.pdf), 2023, official; [Ofcom guidance note](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0021/16248/gn_flash.pdf), 2001, official `dated` (still cited in breach findings).

### 4. Sound
- When sound and picture drift apart, viewers notice audio that arrives early sooner than audio that arrives late. ITU-R BT.1359 puts detection at about 45 ms early or 125 ms late, and acceptability at about 90 ms early or 185 ms late. EBU R37 asks productions to stay within 40 ms early and 60 ms late. **Deadeye:** put the swish on the exact frame the ball enters the net. Up to one frame late is fine (33 ms at 30 fps). Never early. [Wikipedia summary of BT.1359/R37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio-to-video_synchronization), secondary; [EBU R37](https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/r037), official.
- In a sync study (20 viewers, 45 clips), viewers tolerated more offset in complex sports events than in speech. **Deadeye:** voice and lip-sync need the tightest timing. Ball SFX have a little more room, but keep to the rule above. [Ebenezer, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01686), 2022, study (preprint).
- In experiments, adding crowd noise to sports broadcasts made viewers rate the play as more exciting. **Deadeye:** a gym-ambience lift or a small crowd swell on the make adds energy. Don't fake a crowd that implies an event that didn't happen. [Cummins et al., J. Broadcasting & Electronic Media, via TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2020-09-professor-artificial-crowd-noise-applicable.amp), 2019, study (secondary).
- Viewers preferred frequent sync between musical and visual accents (small study). In an analysis of 329 music videos, about two-thirds sync at the beat level. Music that fits the ad aids recall, and music that doesn't fit distracts. **Deadeye:** land cuts, banner hits and the count tick on beats. Pick music that fits a grind-and-effort mood. [Rogers & Gibson, ICMPC](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/14946/1/ICMPC2012_AndrewRogers.pdf), 2012, study; [music-video analysis, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.00970), 2021, study; [Oakes, JAR](https://www.warc.com/content/article/jar/evaluating-empirical-research-into-music-in-advertising-a-congruity-perspective/en-gb/85557), 2007, study (review).
- Meta reports that Reels campaigns with music or voiceover show "up to 13% higher incremental conversions". Apple suggests one continuous score across a preview, plus real UI sounds. **Deadeye:** one music bed plus real-feeling SFX. No silent promos. [Social Media Today on Meta](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182), 2025, industry; [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/), official.
- Loudness: TikTok and Instagram publish no loudness target. Practitioner consensus is about -14 LUFS integrated with true peak at or below -1 dBTP. AES TD1008 suggests distribution loudness of about -18 LUFS for speech, -17 for sport or mixed content and -16 for music (secondary). EBU R128 s1 limits short-form spikes through a maximum short-term loudness. **Deadeye:** master to -14 LUFS, true peak -1.5 dBTP, and keep short-term peaks within about +5 LU of integrated so SFX hits don't jump out. [OpenClip summary](https://openclip.app/learn/audio-normalization), practitioner; [Telos on TD1008](https://docs.telosalliance.com/docs/understanding-loudness-for-streaming-audio), practitioner; [EBU R128 s1](https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128s1.pdf), official.
- Synthetic voice: in a 1,300-person test, human voices scored about 23% higher on trust than AI voices, and labelling a voice as AI cut trust by about 27%. In a separate study, fewer than half of listeners could identify generic AI voices. **Deadeye:** use Kokoro only when a human voice isn't available, keep it plain (no fake hype), and let text carry the message. [Studio Resonate via ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/studio-resonate-ai-voice-study/), about 2025, industry (vendor); [WPP Media](https://www.wppmedia.com/news/ai-voices-audio-ads), industry.
- Disclosure: YouTube requires disclosure of realistic synthetic content, such as making a real person appear to say something they didn't, but not caption creation or audio repair. It doesn't address a generic narrator directly. TikTok requires labels on realistic AI content, and ads have an AI-content toggle (secondary). EU AI Act Article 50 applies from 2 Aug 2026 and requires clear disclosure of deepfake audio that resembles real people. **Deadeye:** never clone or imitate a real person's voice. Whether to tick the AI label for a generic TTS narrator is a founder decision (see Open questions). [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491), current, official; [McCann FitzGerald on Art. 50](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/ai-transparency-european-commissions-guidelines-on-article-50-part-2-deployer-obligations), 2026, practitioner (law firm); [Stellar Search on TikTok ads](https://www.stellarsearch.co.uk/insight/tiktoks-ai-ad-disclosure-rules-are-live-what-brands-running-ai-creative-need-to-do-now), practitioner.

### 5. Delivery specs and recompression
- TikTok ads (June 2026): 9:16 at 540x960 or more, MP4/MOV, up to 500 MB, at least 516 kbps. No frame-rate or audio spec is given. TikTok's creative page asks for at least 720p. **Deadeye:** deliver 1080x1920. [TikTok ad specs](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/video-ads-specifications?lang=en), official.
- Meta Reels ads: 9:16, 1440x2560 recommended, H.264, fixed frame rate, square pixels, progressive scan, stereo AAC at 128 kbps or more, up to 4 GB. Meta also advises against licensed music in ads (use original audio or its Sound Collection). **Deadeye:** for Meta ads, render a 1440x2560 version only when the source has the pixels. [Meta Reels ad spec](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels), official.
- YouTube's upload spec is the most detailed published one: MP4 with the moov atom at the front, no edit lists, H.264 High profile, progressive, 2 B-frames, closed GOP of half the frame rate, CABAC, 4:2:0, AAC-LC at 48 kHz, BT.709 tags, frame rate kept as recorded, and 8 Mbps for 1080p30. YouTube converts full-range video to limited range. **Deadeye:** use this as the house spec for every platform. [YouTube encoding settings](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171), current, official.
- iPhone HDR clips (HLG or Dolby Vision) have looked washed out on Instagram because the HDR metadata was stripped. Meta added Dolby Vision support on iOS in Nov 2025. YouTube converts BT.2020 video without an HDR transfer to BT.709. **Deadeye:** tone-map every HDR source to SDR BT.709 before editing, so the overlay colours match the footage. [India TV News](https://www.indiatvnews.com/technology/news/instagram-for-ios-finally-adds-full-dolby-vision-hdr-support-what-changes-for-users-2025-11-19-1018177), 2025, practitioner (news).
- In ffmpeg's x264 guide, CRF 18 is "visually lossless" and the default is 23; +6 CRF roughly halves the bitrate. Use the slowest preset you can tolerate. **Deadeye:** platforms re-encode everything, so upload a near-lossless master (CRF 16-18). [FFmpeg H.264 guide](https://trac.ffmpeg.org/wiki/Encode/H.264), official (project wiki).
- On VMAF, a gap of about 6 points is roughly a just-noticeable difference, and a phone viewing model exists. **Deadeye:** use VMAF to compare your master against a simulated low-bitrate platform re-encode. If detail collapses (fine net, small UI text), simplify the frame. [Streaming Learning Center](https://streaminglearningcenter.com/encoding/the-vmaf-phone-model-and-saving-on-streaming-to-mobile-viewers.html), industry.

### 6. Showing the app on screen
- Apple's preview rules: use footage captured on the device, keep transitions from implying features the app lacks, and remember previews play muted, so add copy and keep text "on the screen long enough for people to read". Pick a strong poster frame and use real UI sound effects. **Deadeye:** every MAKE/MISS, count and replay shown must be real app output, never animated to look better than the app performs. [Apple app previews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-previews/), official.
- Google Play wants the core experience shown within the first 10 s, at least 80% of the video showing the real experience, no black bars on portrait video, legible text, and no "Download now" or performance claims. It allows filming people only when the core use happens off the device. **Deadeye:** Deadeye's core use is shooting on court, so the player belongs on screen. Put the overlay on real shots by second 1-2. [Google Play preview videos](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151), official.
- HomeCourt, the nearest comparable app, was shown at Apple's 2018 iPhone keynote with live shot tracking drawn over real shooting. **Deadeye:** a phone on a tripod plus the live overlay is a proven way to show this kind of product. [CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nba/news/apple-iphone-xs-event-steve-nash-homecourt-basketball-training-app), 2018, practitioner (news).

### 7. Authenticity, polish and colour
- In a 2x2 experiment (user-made vs agency-made ads, low vs high technical quality), user-made videos beat agency videos on trust and expertise at low quality. At high quality, the source made no difference to trust. **Deadeye:** raw gym footage works when it looks like a player shot it. Half-polished brand footage gets the worst of both. [Hautz et al., J. Interactive Marketing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/joinma/v28y2014i1p1-15.html), 2014, study.
- Higher production quality raised viewers' sense of an influencer's expertise but not their trust. **Deadeye:** polish helps sound, framing, legibility and exposure. It doesn't create trust. [Phys.org on Johnson et al.](https://phys.org/news/2021-04-beauty-gurus-trustworthy-audience-production.html), 2021, study (secondary).
- In Realeyes attention and emotion tests across 11 categories, UGC-style TikToks scored 22% above brand TikToks and 46% above conventional ads. TikTok's own Creative Center reports that 65% of users find overly professional brand videos out of place (read in a search summary, because the page returned an error). **Deadeye:** keep the phone-camera look and grade only to fix problems (exposure, white balance, HDR). [Realeyes blog](https://blog.realeyesit.com/tiktok-ugc-and-brand-videos-outperform-other-ad-formats-in-driving-attention-in-mobile) (now redirects), industry (vendor, undated, method not shown).
- I found no study on grading amateur gym footage (backlit windows, mixed lighting). **Deadeye:** measure, don't guess. ffmpeg `signalstats` gives per-frame YAVG, YLOW and YHIGH, so you can spot a shot much darker or brighter than its neighbours and match it. [FFmpeg filters](https://www.ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html), official.

### 8. QA without watching
- ffmpeg's `scdet` logs scene-change times; its docs say thresholds of 8-14 work well. PySceneDetect's AdaptiveDetector (rolling average) is recommended when the camera moves fast. **Deadeye:** run both on the render and compare the detected cuts with the planned edit list. Extra cuts point to glitches, and missing ones point to dropped clips. [FFmpeg filters](https://www.ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html), official; [PySceneDetect](https://www.scenedetect.com/docs/latest/api/detectors.html), official (project docs).
- ffmpeg can also measure black frames (`blackdetect`), frozen frames (`freezedetect`), silences (`silencedetect`), loudness and true peak (`ebur128`), and exposure (`signalstats`). **Deadeye:** every render gets these numbers before anyone watches it. [FFmpeg filters](https://www.ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html), official.
- PEAT, the Trace Center's free flash-analysis tool, tests WCAG flash rules but "is NOT designed to analyze commercial videos"; broadcast work uses the commercial Harding FPA. **Deadeye:** run a home-made luminance and red-flash counter on every render, and use PEAT as an extra check. [Phys.org on PEAT](https://phys.org/news/2009-07-software-tool-web-seizure-causing-content.html), 2009, practitioner `dated`.
- What can't be measured: Murch ranks emotion first and story second for a cut (51% and 23% of his weighting), and no metric captures either. **Deadeye:** no clip ships until the founder has watched it once at full speed with sound on and once with sound off. [No Film School on Murch](https://nofilmschool.com/2016/11/6-rules-good-cutting-according-oscar-winning-editor-walter-murch), practitioner (secondary on a 1995 textbook).

### 9. Sports advertising
- In the IPA databank (996 campaigns), emotional campaigns produced more brand and business effects over the long term than rational ones. Rational messages drove short-term sales. The best results combined both. **Deadeye:** lead with feeling (effort, a breakthrough) and add one rational proof point (the real make count). [IPA Long and Short of It](https://ipa.co.uk/media/14463/long_and_short_of_it_presentation_final_030424.pdf), 2013, industry (award entries, self-selected).
- System1's Star Rating is built from viewers' emotional response. The average Super Bowl ad scores only 3 of 5 stars. Ads with brand characters or familiar brand assets scored about a point higher than celebrity-led ads. **Deadeye:** build recurring assets (the MAKE banner, the counter sound, the brand colour) instead of chasing big names. [WARC on System1](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/celebrity-saturation-fails-in-super-bowl-ads/43249), 2020, industry; [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/412670/two-super-bowl-ads-from-nfl-tops-in-creative-effec.html), 2026, industry.
- Underdog brand stories that combine an outside disadvantage with passion and determination increased purchase intent and choice (in one study, 71% chose the underdog brand). The effect was strongest for people who see themselves as underdogs. **Deadeye:** the effort arc (a cold gym, missed shots, a kid who keeps going) suits most teen players. [Paharia et al., J. Consumer Research](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/doi10.1086-656219.html), 2011, study.
- Well-known short sports spots, in my own words. Nike "Jogger" (2012) is one unbroken take of an ordinary boy running toward camera, with a voiceover and the brand only at the end. Under Armour "Rule Yourself" (2016) is a dark, nearly wordless montage of unseen training (ice baths, weights, sleep), driven by sound design and closing on a line about work done in the dark. It won the Cannes Film Craft Grand Prix and drew 308k shares. Under Armour "I Will What I Want" (2014) sets a voice reading a real rejection letter against the athlete performing: doubt in the audio, proof in the picture. Nike "Dream Crazy" (2018) is a montage of escalating real athlete stories under one voiceover. **Deadeye:** these show that one strong device beats many tricks (single take, sound-led grind, audio-versus-picture contrast). [Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/nike-find-greatness-jogger-wieden-kennedy-portland/1144477); [Quartz](https://qz.com/758289/the-moving-michael-phelps-ad-that-has-been-gold-for-under-armour-at-the-2016-rio-olympics); [Unruly](https://unruly.co/?p=11730); [TIME](https://time.com/3083114/misty-copeland-under-armour-i-will-what-i-want/); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/colin-kaepernick-narrates-and-stars-nike-s-dream-crazy-film), practitioner. Sales claims for these campaigns are unverified.
- A survey of 218 adolescents found athlete role models influence teens' purchase intentions and word of mouth. **Deadeye:** use players who look like the audience; I found no evidence that same-age players beat pros. [Bush, Martin & Bush, JAR](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jadres/v44y2004i01p108-118_04.html), 2004, study.
- The Irish ASAI code treats anyone under 18 as a child and has a dedicated children's section. **Deadeye:** no pressure-to-buy lines, no exaggerated results, nothing unsafe, and check the current ASAI children's section before any paid ad. [William Fry on the ASAI code](https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/updated-asai-code/), 2016, practitioner (law firm) `dated`.

### 10. Sports video on social
- 38% of Gen Z sports fans watch highlights weekly, about the same share that watch live sport. In a 2025 survey of 1,050 US fans, 39% of Gen Z said short highlights (under 2 minutes) matched or beat live viewing. **Deadeye:** Deadeye promos compete with highlight clips, so they need highlight energy. [Morning Consult](https://pro.morningconsult.com/articles/gen-z-future-of-live-sports), industry; [WSC Sports study](https://lp.wsc-sports.com/hubfs/The%202025%20WSC%20Sports%20Fan%20Engagement%20Study%20on%20How%20Gen%20X%2c%20Y%2c%20and%20Z%20Are%20Redefining%20Sports%20Fandom%20(1).pdf), 2025, industry (vendor).
- House of Highlights succeeded with a "raw and relatable" feel, and its owner said polished branded video wouldn't work there. Overtime grew by posting short clips of high-school athletes. The NBA's TikTok runs top moments natively in vertical. **Deadeye:** teen athletes are the heroes and the look stays raw. [Digiday on HoH](https://digiday.com/media/bleacher-reports-aims-to-turn-house-of-highlights-into-a-business); [Digiday on Overtime](https://digiday.com/media/how-overtime-is-building-sports-media-for-gen-z); [Hashtag Sports on @NBA TikTok](https://www.hashtagsports.com/awards/shortlist-2024/nba-tiktok), practitioner.
- Common conventions, observed rather than tested: open cold on the best play; speed-ramp into the release; push crowd or gym noise on big plays; let impact SFX cut through the music; replay the key moment. **Deadeye:** use them, but treat them as conventions, not evidence. [Premiere Pro football-edit guide](https://grandavehousing.calpoly.edu/blog/master-football-edits-in-adobe-premiere-pro-1764799973), practitioner.

### 11. Editing craft
- Murch's "rule of six" ranks what a cut must serve: emotion 51%, story 23%, rhythm 10%, eye-trace 7%, the 2D screen plane 5%, 3D space 4%. Give up the lower items first. **Deadeye:** a slightly mismatched cut that keeps the feeling beats a clean cut that kills it. [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2016/11/6-rules-good-cutting-according-oscar-winning-editor-walter-murch), practitioner (secondary on *In the Blink of an Eye*).
- Smith's attentional theory explains continuity editing (match-action, eye-line, point-of-view, the 180-degree rule) as cues that steer attention across the cut: motion, gaze, pointing and off-screen sound. **Deadeye:** before a cut, point attention where the next shot's key item will appear (eye-trace). [Smith, Projections](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/), 2012, study (theory).
- A careful replication (36 viewers) found some Kuleshov effect is real: the shot next to a neutral face shifts the emotion people read into it. **Deadeye:** a neutral face cut after a rising counter reads as pride, and after a miss it reads as resolve. [Barratt et al., Perception](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27056181/), 2016, study.
- Pearlman describes editing rhythm as timing, pacing and "trajectory phrasing", the movement energy carried within and between shots, used to shape tension and release. **Deadeye:** build tension with shorter shots and rising sound, then release on a held payoff shot. [Pearlman, *Cutting Rhythms*](https://www.routledge.com/Cutting-Rhythms-Creative-Film-Editing/Pearlman/p/book/9781041024088), 3rd ed. 2025, textbook.
- Material Design's motion timings: elements entering take about 225 ms and slow to a stop, elements leaving take about 195 ms and speed up, and anything over 400 ms feels slow. **Deadeye:** text and banners pop in fast, ease out, and don't drift. [Material Design v1](https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html), UI guidance not video, official `dated`.

## Craft playbook
- **Cold open on proof.** When: always. Why: TikTok and Meta both put the hook in the first seconds, and Nielsen's data puts much of the effect in the first 3 s. How: frame 0 is already mid-action (the shot going up) with the overlay visible; the MAKE banner fires by 1-2 s; no logo card first.
- **Real speed, then replay.** When: a key make. Why: slow-mo adds a sense of skill (Caruso), and showing real speed first keeps it honest. How: play the shot at 1x, then cut to a 0.4-0.5x replay of the release and swish on 60 fps or faster footage, with a whoosh into the slow section.
- **Hide cuts on action.** When: joining two angles or two shots of one drill. Why: about a third of cuts made on motion go unseen (Smith & Henderson). How: cut at the jump or release, keep the ambience bed running, and land the new shot's subject near the old one's screen position.
- **Make a cut felt.** When: switching from footage to the stat card or to the replay. Why: removing post-cut motion and changing the sound makes a cut noticeable (Smith 2017). How: a hard cut on a beat, a sound change at the cut, and a static or still first frame after it.
- **J-cuts and L-cuts.** When: a voiceover or crowd sound spans a cut. Why: continuous audio smooths the cut (Smith 2017). How: start the next shot's sound 4-8 frames before its picture (J), or let the swish ring 4-8 frames into the next shot (L). The frame counts are a rule of thumb.
- **Speed ramp.** When: an athletic peak (jump, release). How: 100% to 30-50% across 6-10 frames at the apex, back to 100% for the landing, with a whoosh starting slightly before the ramp because it is anticipatory. Use high-frame-rate sources only.
- **Punch-in.** When: to show the counter or banner, or to add emphasis on a beat. How: only within the pixel budget (section 2), with a cut-zoom on a beat rather than a slow digital creep on a 1080p source.
- **Freeze frame plus text.** When: one fact needs reading time, such as "47/60 this week". Why: reading speed limits (section 3). How: freeze on a strong pose, hold for (characters / 17) s + 0.5 s, keep the text inside the safe zone, and unfreeze on a beat.
- **Beat grid, not a metronome.** When: montage sections. Why: viewers like accents in sync (Rogers & Gibson), and good films vary shot length (Cutting). How: put cuts on beats, but mix 1-, 2- and 4-beat shot lengths and give the payoff the longest hold.
- **Reaction shot (Kuleshov).** When: you have a face. How: put the stat or the make first, then the player's face for 0.5-1 s, so the viewer reads pride into it.
- **Effort arc in 15-20 s.** When: brand pieces. Why: the underdog effect (Paharia) and emotion over reason (Binet & Field). How: struggle (a miss, a cold gym, early hours), then work (a fast montage of reps with the count rising), then proof (a real make streak with the overlay), then brand and app name. The voiceover or text speaks to the player as "you".
- **One device, not ten.** Why: the strong sports spots in section 9 each lean on one device. How: pick a single take, a sound-led grind, or an audio-versus-picture contrast, and keep effects to two per video.
- **Captions.** Chunk text at phrase boundaries, use at most 2 lines and 42 characters, highlight the active word only when it is spoken, use a heavy sans with a stroke or plate, and put nothing in the bottom 35%.
- **Text motion.** In: about 200-250 ms with a decelerating ease. Out: about 150-200 ms with an accelerating ease. No more than one moving text element at a time; two or more moving at once is my rule of thumb, since I found no study on it.
- **SFX layer.** Swish on the net-contact frame, sneaker squeak on the plant, a rim clank on misses, the counter tick on the count change, and ambience under everything. Keep each hit 0-1 frame late, never early.
- **Loop seam.** When: a video meant to loop. How: match the last frame's composition and motion to frame 0, and end the audio on a phrase that leads back into the opening. There is no evidence this helps (see Open questions).
- **End card.** The app name and logo were already shown early. Close on a real UI shot plus the name, inside the safe zone, held for at least 1.5 s. On Google Play videos, don't use "Download now".

## Checklist candidates
1. The frame at 0.0 s shows the player in motion with the tracking overlay visible (TikTok 3 s guidance, Google Play's first-10-s rule).
2. The app name or brand is readable on screen before 3 s (Nielsen/Facebook brand lift).
3. Every text box sits inside x 65-1015 and y 269-1248 on 1080x1920, and inside TikTok's safe-zone file for the planned caption length (Meta and TikTok specs).
4. Every caption has at most 42 characters per line and 2 lines, and text that must be read alone runs at 17 characters/s or less (Netflix children's limit).
5. Worst-case contrast between caption and background, including the stroke or plate, is at least 4.5:1 on sampled stills (WCAG 1.4.3).
6. No 1-s window has more than 3 large-area luminance flashes or more than 3 saturated-red flashes (WCAG 2.3.1, Ofcom, BT.1702).
7. Each SFX onset falls 0-33 ms after its event frame and is never early (BT.1359, EBU R37).
8. Integrated loudness is -14 LUFS ±1, true peak is -1.5 dBTP or lower, and maximum short-term loudness is no more than about 5 LU above integrated (practitioner consensus, R128 s1).
9. `scdet` cut times match the planned edit list, with no unplanned cuts (ffmpeg docs).
10. `blackdetect` and `freezedetect` find nothing that wasn't planned (ffmpeg docs).
11. Cut rate in talky sections is about 0.2-0.5 cuts/s (Dost & Huang benchmark).
12. Every slow-motion shot comes from a source of at least 1/(speed) x 30 fps, or is marked as interpolated (frame arithmetic).
13. No punch-in drops below 1:1 source pixels per output pixel beyond the agreed limit (pixel arithmetic, Apple's native-resolution advice).
14. ffprobe shows h264 High, yuv420p, bt709 primaries/trc/matrix, tv range, constant frame rate, AAC-LC 48 kHz stereo and moov at the front (YouTube and Meta specs).
15. No source was HDR (arib-std-b67 or smpte2084), or it was tone-mapped to BT.709 (Instagram HDR issue).
16. Every MAKE/MISS, count and replay comes from real app output (Apple: transitions mustn't imply missing features; ASAI honesty).
17. VMAF between the master and a simulated low-bitrate platform re-encode doesn't fall more than about 6 points below the same test on previous approved videos (VMAF JND).
18. A stills contact sheet (frame 0, mid-point of every shot, every text-on, last frame) has been reviewed; then a human watches with sound on and off (Murch: emotion and story can't be measured).

## ffmpeg and delivery recipe
- **Probe sources:** `ffprobe -v error -show_entries stream=codec_name,width,height,r_frame_rate,avg_frame_rate,pix_fmt,color_range,color_space,color_transfer,color_primaries -of json in.mov`. A transfer of `arib-std-b67` (HLG) or `smpte2084` (PQ) means HDR, and a mismatch between `r_frame_rate` and `avg_frame_rate` means variable frame rate. Why: iPhone HDR footage and variable-rate phone video cause most colour and sync problems later.
- **Normalise sources first** to constant frame rate and SDR: `-vf "zscale=t=linear:npl=100,format=gbrpf32le,zscale=p=bt709,tonemap=tonemap=hable:desat=0,zscale=t=bt709:m=bt709:r=tv,format=yuv420p" -fps_mode cfr -r 30`. Why: ffmpeg's tonemap doc says tone-mapping must happen in linear light with zscale around it; Hable is the common choice (practitioner). This needs ffmpeg built with libzimg, so check `ffmpeg -filters | findstr zscale`. Older builds use `-vsync cfr` instead of `-fps_mode cfr`. Keep 60 fps sources at 60 until speed-ramping is done.
- **Scale:** `scale=1080:1920:flags=lanczos+accurate_rnd+full_chroma_int`. Why: Lanczos (3 taps) is the sharpest built-in resampler. The newest ffmpeg docs mark `flags=lanczos` as deprecated in favour of `scaler=lanczos`, so check `ffmpeg -h filter=scale` on the installed version. Only downscale; don't upscale beyond the pixel budget.
- **Sharpen (optional):** a mild `unsharp=5:5:0.3:5:5:0` only after downscaling from 4K, and never on upscaled footage. Why: it may help fine detail (the net, UI text) survive platform re-encoding. Untested: keep it only if the stills and the VMAF check (checklist 17) look better.
- **Master encode:** `-c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 17 -profile:v high -pix_fmt yuv420p -g 15 -bf 2 -fps_mode cfr -r 30 -color_primaries bt709 -color_trc bt709 -colorspace bt709 -color_range tv -movflags +faststart`. Why: CRF 17-18 is near "visually lossless" (FFmpeg guide); High profile, 2 B-frames, a closed GOP of half the frame rate, 4:2:0, BT.709 tags, limited range and fast start are YouTube's published spec; Meta asks for a fixed frame rate and progressive scan. Use `-g 30` at 60 fps. Optionally add `-maxrate 20M -bufsize 40M` to cap spikes; that cap is a precaution, since no platform publishes a maximum bitrate.
- **Audio:** `-c:a aac -b:a 256k -ar 48000 -ac 2`. Why: YouTube specifies AAC-LC at 48 kHz, and Meta asks for stereo AAC at 128 kbps or more. 256-384k leaves room for the platform's own re-encode.
- **Loudness, two passes:**
  - Pass 1: `ffmpeg -i mix.wav -af loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1.5:LRA=11:print_format=json -f null -`.
  - Pass 2: `-af loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1.5:LRA=11:measured_I=..:measured_TP=..:measured_LRA=..:measured_thresh=..:offset=..:linear=true:print_format=json -ar 48000`.
  - Then confirm `normalization_type` is `linear` in the pass-2 JSON. The ffmpeg docs say loudnorm falls back to dynamic mode when target LRA < source LRA or when the gain would break the true-peak target. If that happens, raise LRA or limit the peaks before normalising.
  - Always set `-ar 48000`, because dynamic mode upsamples to 192 kHz.
- **Verify loudness:** `ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -af ebur128=peak=true -f null -` and read I, LRA, true peak and the short-term maximum (checklist 8).
- **QA pass on the render:**
  - `ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -vf "scdet=t=10,blackdetect=d=0.1,freezedetect=n=-60dB:d=0.5,signalstats,metadata=print:file=qa.txt" -af silencedetect=n=-50dB:d=0.4 -f null -`
  - Then pull stills: `ffmpeg -ss T -i out.mp4 -frames:v 1 -q:v 2 t_T.png` at frame 0, the mid-point of every shot, every text-on and the last frame.
  - Why: these are documented ffmpeg filters, and `scdet` thresholds of 8-14 are the documented good range.
- **Compression stress test:**
  - Re-encode the master at a low bitrate, e.g. `-b:v 3M`. That figure is a guess at platform delivery rates, since none publish them.
  - Then score it: `ffmpeg -i lowbr.mp4 -i master.mp4 -lavfi libvmaf -f null -`. This needs ffmpeg built with libvmaf.
  - Why: it shows which elements (thin net, small UI text, grain) break first, and a 6-point VMAF gap is about one just-noticeable difference.
- **Per-platform outputs:** TikTok and organic Reels or Shorts get 1080x1920. Meta paid Reels get 1440x2560 only when the source is 4K. Keep the duration at or under 30 s so it also works as an Apple app preview.

## Open questions
- Ideal length for an app-demo promo: the "21-34 s" TikTok figure can't be traced to a primary source, and nothing covers app demos specifically.
- Cut rate for sports or action montages: the only short-form benchmark (Dost & Huang) covers talking-head videos.
- Word-by-word (karaoke) captions against phrase captions in short-form: I found no direct study. TikTok's "5-10 words per second" conflicts with subtitle standards.
- Reading speed of 13-17-year-olds for on-screen text in fast video: the only figure is Netflix's 17 characters/s for children's programmes.
- How many text elements one frame can hold: only limited-capacity theory (Lang, cited by Dost & Huang), with no numbers.
- Actual loudness normalisation and re-encode bitrates on TikTok and Instagram: unpublished, so -14 LUFS rests on practitioner consensus.
- Whether 1440x2560 uploads help organic Instagram Reels (Meta recommends it for ads only).
- Loop seams and rewatches: no reliable evidence that seamless loops raise completion or rewatch rates.
- Whether grading amateur gym footage helps or hurts ad performance; only indirect evidence (Hautz et al., Realeyes).
- Sports-social conventions (cold open on the best play, POV and challenge formats, ramps): widely used but untested. No published editing data from NBA, Overtime, House of Highlights or HomeCourt.
- Whether teen audiences respond better to same-age amateur players than to pros: not found.
- Disclosure for a generic Kokoro TTS narrator: YouTube and TikTok policies don't clearly cover it, and EU AI Act Art. 50 deepfake duties likely don't apply to a voice that imitates no real person. The trust penalty when AI is disclosed (about -27%) suggests a founder decision on labelling.
- Primary texts not read directly: ITU-R BT.1359 and EBU R37 (numbers from secondary sources), Murch's percentages, AES TD1008 targets, the full ASAI children's section in its current edition, and the Meta research behind "+13% conversions with music/voiceover".
